Understanding the Science

Transcranial Direct Current Stimulation

Transcranial Direct Current Stimulation (tDCS) is a form of non-invasive brain stimulation that uses a low electrical current delivered through electrodes placed on the scalp. The technique modulates neuronal activity by applying a constant, low-intensity direct current to specific areas of the brain.

The Process

How Does tDCS Work?

Electrode Placement

Small electrodes are placed on the scalp at specific locations based on the targeted brain region. The positioning is guided by standardized protocols to ensure consistent and safe stimulation delivery.

Low Current Delivery

A mild direct current (typically 1–2 milliamps) flows between the electrodes, modulating brain activity. The current is not strong enough to cause neurons to fire but can shift their resting membrane potential.

Neural Modulation

The stimulation can increase or decrease neuronal excitability depending on electrode polarity, which may help regulate pain processing pathways and improve pain-related symptoms over time.
